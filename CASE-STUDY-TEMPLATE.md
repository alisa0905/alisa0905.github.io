# Case study template

Copy everything below the line for each project, fill it in, and send it to Claude.
Each part maps to something on the case-study page, so it can go straight onto the site.

**Rules of thumb (from the sources at the bottom):**
- Start with the problem, not the deliverables. "Their Shopify site looked like every other store" beats "I designed a website".
- Say what *you* did. On team projects, say who did what.
- Show the thinking, not just the final work: one early sketch, rejected direction or moodboard does a lot.
- Recruiters skim. Short paragraphs, one idea each. Aim for 150–300 words per case study.
- End with what happened and what you learned. Numbers if you have them, honest reactions if you don't.
- Pick 4–6 projects you're proudest of, and the kind of work you want more of. Cut the rest.

**1. The situation** (2–3 sentences)
Who is the client, and what was wrong or missing before you started?
> e.g. Cloud Castle sold clothes through a stock Shopify template that looked like thousands of other stores.

**2. The goal** (1–2 sentences)
What did they need to happen? Who was it for?
> e.g. A storefront that felt like the brand, for customers who discover it on Instagram.

**3. What I did, and why** (3–5 short points)
Your main decisions and the reason behind each. This is the most important part.
- I did ___ because ___.
- I did ___ because ___.
- I chose ___ over ___ because ___.

**4. The process** (pick 1–2 images to show)
Research, moodboard, sketches, an early version, a rejected idea. What changed between the first idea and the final one?

**5. The result** (2–3 sentences)
What shipped, and what happened after: numbers, client reaction, still in use, approved first time…
> e.g. Live at cloudcastle.co. Approved with minimal revisions.

**6. What I learned** (1–2 sentences, optional)
Something you'd do differently, or a skill this project gave you.
